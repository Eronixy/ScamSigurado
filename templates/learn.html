Common Types of Online Scams ⚠️

Scammers keep finding new ways to trick people, but by knowing their methods, you can stop them.

Task and Fake Job Offer Scams

These scams usually appear as messages or posts promising easy money or high-paying online jobs. They might claim you can earn at least ₱500 a day by liking pages, sharing posts, or doing simple "tasks." Some also pretend to be recruiters offering home-based jobs with big salaries. At first, they might give you small payments or show fake proofs of earnings to make you believe it's real. Then, they'll ask you to pay a "registration," "activation," or "training" fee to continue—once you send the money, they stop replying or block you.

How to stay safe:

  • Never pay to join an earning or job program.
  • Be cautious of offers that sound too easy or too good to be true.
  • Check if the company or page is verified or registered with DOLE.
  • Search online for reviews or complaints before trusting any "opportunity."

Phishing Scam

You receive a text claiming your account is locked and asking you to click a suspicious link to "verify". These links are designed to steal your passwords or OTPs. Once you enter your details, scammers can access your account.

How to stay safe:

  • Never click on suspicious links.
  • Always check the official bank number or email.
  • banks never ask for passwords or OTPs.

Gambling Scam

These scams usually appear as online betting or casino platforms that promise guaranteed wins or "risk-free" gambling. Some even show fake screenshots of people "winning" thousands of pesos to make it look legitimate. Once you deposit money, they either rig the game so you always lose or block your account entirely.

How to stay safe:

  • Only use licensed and verified gambling sites regulated by PAGCOR.
  • Avoid pages or messages that promise "sure wins" or ask you to deposit through GCash or personal accounts.
  • Remember: real gambling is never guaranteed — if it sounds too easy, it's likely a scam.

How to Know If It's a Scam 🚩

Look for these Red Flags:

  • Too good to be true: If it sounds unbelievable, it probably is.
  • Poor grammar or spelling: Many scam messages have awkward or broken English/Tagalog.
  • Pressure tactics: Scammers make you act fast "limited offer" or "urgent."
  • Requests for money: Legit companies or contests never ask for fees.
  • Unverified sources: Check if the message is from an official account.

Practice Spotting a Scam 🧠

Let's test your awareness. Read the messages below and click "Reveal Answer":

Message 1

"Congratulations! You've won P10,000. Click this link to claim your prize: bit.ly/claimnow"

That's a scam.

Real contests contact winners through official channels, not random text messages or short links.

Message 2

"Hi, this is your bank. Please verify your account details so we can unlock it."

Also a scam.

Your bank will never ask for personal details or passwords via SMS or social media.

Message 3

"Hi, I'm recruiting part-time data encoders. No fees needed, just internet access and time."

Legit, but still double-check.

Since they are not asking for money, the job itself might be legitimate. Always check for a proper job posting or company profile before applying.

Simple Habits to Stay Protected 🔒

Be ScamAlam with these simple tips:

  • Protect your OTP and passwords: Don't share your OTP or passwords not even with family.
  • Use strong security: Use strong passwords and enable two-factor authentication (2FA) on all accounts.
  • Set privacy: Keep your social media profiles private.
  • Pause before you click: Before clicking a link, pause and think: "Is this safe?"
  • Use ScamAlam: Scan suspicious screenshots or messages with the app to check for threats.
